## 1. The one piece of arithmetic that matters

JEE Main Paper 1: 75 questions, 300 marks, +4 for right, **−1 for wrong**, nothing for skipped.

| Strategy | Attempted | Right | Wrong | Score |
|---|---|---|---|---|
| Current typical "average" pattern | 60 | 35 | 25 | **115** |
| Panic-attempt everything | 75 | 45 | 30 | **150** |
| **Disciplined** | **45** | **40** | **5** | **155** |
| Disciplined + 3 more right | 48 | 43 | 5 | **167** |

Read the first and third rows again. **The disciplined student knows less and scores 40 marks more.**
That is the entire opportunity. For an average student, 66 days is not enough time to learn 25 new
chapters, but it is more than enough to stop donating 25 marks a paper to wrong answers.

A wrong answer costs you 5 marks relative to a skip (−1 versus +4). Guessing at 25% odds is a losing
bet unless you can eliminate two options. **Skipping is free. Say it out loud until he believes it.**

## 2. Where the marks are, ranked by return per hour

For an average student, in this order:

1. **Accuracy discipline** — worth 25–40 marks. Free. Costs nothing but honesty.
2. **Locking the 38 Tier-A chapters** (see `data/syllabus.csv`) — worth 40+ marks. These are the
   chapters that appear every year and are learnable to near-100% in one sitting each.
3. **Section B numericals** — 15 questions, 60 marks, and most average students quietly lose 12–20
   marks here. These are not harder, just less practised, and there is no option-elimination escape.
   Practise numericals in every chapter, not just MCQs.
4. **Time strategy / three passes** — worth 10–15 marks. Trained by doing mocks under time, not by
   reading about time management.
5. **New hard chapters** — worth 5–10 marks for 30+ hours. Rotational dynamics, deep electrostatics,
   oxygen-containing organic chemistry. *Last priority, not first.* Students instinctively do this
   first because it feels like progress. It is the worst available use of the next three weeks.

## 3. The three phases

| Phase | Dates | Days | What it is |
|---|---|---|---|
| **P1 — Triage** | 15 Nov – 6 Dec | 22 | Tier-A chapters only, ~2 a day, each finished with 15 PYQs. One mock a week to track. Nothing is "revised" yet; it is learned properly once. |
| **P2 — Consolidate** | 7 Dec – 3 Jan | 28 | Tier-B chapters + mocks twice a week (Wed, Sun). The error log becomes the main document, not the textbook. |
| **P3 — Mock grind** | 4 – 19 Jan | 16 | No new topics. Three mocks a week, revision only, timed sectionals. Last 3 days: revision, sleep, and nothing else. |

The generated plan (`out/study_plan.html`) spells out every day. Print it. It is deliberately
front-loaded: if a week gets lost to school exams or illness, the loss is absorbed in P1, not in
January.

### Weekly rhythm

- **Every day:** 2 chapters in depth, then ~4 revision blocks of already-known chapters, one timed sectional
  and a mixed PYQ set. Roughly **8 hours**, since he is self-studying with no school or coaching.
- **No fixed rest day is needed** when the day already contains a long lunch and 20-minute breaks — but if two
  mocks in a row go down, take one full day off. That is fatigue, not laziness.
- **Sunday:** full mock 9–12, then **100 minutes of analysis**. The analysis is the part that pays.
- **One evening off per week.** Non-negotiable. A burnt-out student in the last three weeks is worth
  less than a rested one with two fewer chapters.

## 4. Chapter triage

Open `data/syllabus.csv` — all 54 syllabus units (Maths 14, Physics 20, Chemistry 20), each tagged
with tier, difficulty, and how many questions it usually carries. The tracker shows the same list.

- **Tier A (38 units)** — high frequency, learnable fast, mostly formula-and-application. Learn to
  90% accuracy. This is where the score comes from.
- **Tier B (16 units)** — high frequency but heavy. One careful pass, no perfectionism.
- **Chemistry is the fastest lever** for an average student: 14 of its 20 units are Tier A, and
  inorganic + physical chemistry reward memory and formula recall over deep problem-solving.
  This is why the plan gives Chemistry the first slot each day.

A warning about tier-B traps: coordinate geometry and organic-with-oxygen each carry 2–4 questions
and can eat three weeks. Time-box them. If a chapter has taken two sessions and the PYQs still are
not landing, mark it "shaky" in the tracker and move on. Coming back is cheaper than grinding.

## 5. The error log — the single highest-return habit

One notebook. One line per wrong question, written the same day:

```
date | subject | chapter | what I did wrong | WHY (K/S/T) | correct idea in one line
```

- **K = Knowledge** — did not know the concept. → re-learn the chapter this week.
- **S = Silly** — knew it, rushed, misread, sign error. → this is the 40 marks. Fix by process.
- **T = Time** — knew it, no time. → fix by pass strategy, not by learning.

**Rule: every wrong answer gets an entry, or the mock does not count.** The 20-minute daily slot in
the plan is for re-solving yesterday's log entries cold. If he cannot be bothered to write the log,
he will not improve, and no amount of new material will fix it.

At the end of each week, count the K/S/T split. If S is more than half, the next week's work is
accuracy, not chapters. That single number is the best coaching signal you will get.

## 6. Mock protocol

- Full paper, **9:00–12:00**, same desk, phone in another room. His shift might be the afternoon one,
  so from P3 alternate the start time.
- Practise on a computer at least every other mock — it is a CBT, and scrolling a question on screen
  is its own skill (rough work on paper, careful with the numerical answer box).
- Analysis within 24 hours using `tools/mock_analyzer.py`. It gives marks, an estimated percentile,
  per-subject accuracy, and how many marks the wrong answers cost.
- **Do not chase the percentile.** Chase two numbers: accuracy above 85%, and wrong answers under 8.
- 12–14 mocks before the exam is enough. More than that without analysis is theatre.

## 7. Exam day

**Three passes, 180 minutes.**

1. **Pass 1 (≈70 min):** every question he can finish in under 60 seconds. No exceptions, no
   "let me just finish this one".
2. **Pass 2 (≈70 min):** questions that need 1–3 minutes. Work in order of confidence, not order of
   question number.
3. **Pass 3 (≈30 min):** Section B numericals and anything half-done. Numericals last, because they
   are time sinks and offer no option-elimination shortcut.
4. **Last 10 minutes:** check that every attempted numerical has a value in the box, and that he has
   not left a question with two options marked.

**The guess rule:** no guess unless two options are eliminated. Then guess. Otherwise skip and move on.

**Target:** 45 attempts, 40 right, 5 wrong → **155**. In an average-difficulty shift that is about
**97 percentile, AIR ≈ 42,000**, and it is an achievable target for an average student in 66 days.

## 8. What you, as the uncle, should and should not do

**Do:**
- Own the logistics: form, fee, admit card, exam-centre route, ID, a working watch-free plan.
- Run **one 15-minute review a week** over the analyzer output, with exactly three questions:
  1. What is your accuracy this week? (below 80% → next week is accuracy, not chapters)
  2. What is your K/S/T split? (S > 50% → he is losing marks to process, not knowledge)
  3. What is the one chapter that moved from shaky to OK?
- Protect sleep (7–8 hours) and one evening off. Both are performance features, not indulgences.
- Make the Sunday mock environment boring and consistent. Same desk, same time, no interruptions.
- Tell him about the April session early (see §9) so January stops feeling like a verdict.

**Don't:**
- Teach him. You will teach the way you learned, at 20 years' distance from the syllabus, and it will
  cost you both hours. If a chapter is stuck, the answer is a 20-minute video, not an uncle.
- Buy more material. Adding a fourth question bank is a procrastination technique with a receipt.
- Switch coaching in November. Discontinuity is expensive and this plan is already built around a
  steady weekly rhythm.
- Ask "how many hours did you study today?" It measures nothing and trains him to lie. Ask what he
  fixed instead.
- Compare him to anyone. A rank is not a person.
